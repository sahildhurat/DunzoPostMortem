Quick commerce was winnable — Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart scaled in the same market. Dunzo specifically lost, from a positioning failure (abandoning a differentiated, asset-light category for a capital-heavy commodity war) compounded by a governance failure (capital that removed its ability to course-correct). Unit economics and lost focus were the mechanisms, not the root causes.

Diagnosis

Two root causes, two mechanisms

ROOT CAUSE 01

Positioning failure

Dunzo pioneered and owned hyperlocal errands — the business that made "Dunzo it" a verb. It walked away from that asset-light moat to fight 20-minute grocery as the 4th-funded of four players, where none of its advantages transferred.

ROOT CAUSE 02

Governance failure

It funded the bet with a single dominant strategic investor holding veto rights. When the bet failed, that structure removed both escape hatches — raising fresh capital elsewhere, and selling to an acquirer.

The bottom line

Winnable market. The company lost.

Dunzo led a differentiated category, moved into a commoditised and capital-intensive one where it was structurally disadvantaged, funded the move with capital that removed its ability to recover — and did not survive the gap.
